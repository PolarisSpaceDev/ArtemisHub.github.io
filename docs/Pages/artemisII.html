Artemis II

Artemis II was the first crewed lunar mission since 1972 and the second mission of the Artemis Program. This lunar flyby served as a crewed test flight to validate all life support systems in deep space. This 10 day mission ventured around 4000 miles beyond the far side of the moon making it the furthest humans have ever gone.

History

Formerly known as Exploration Mission 2 and proposed in 2017, this mission would take crew to rendezvous with an astroid that would be placed into lunar orbit from the Astroid Redirect Mission to retrieve samples. After the Astroid Redirect Mission got canceled in 2017, two proposals came up. One proposal would be a free return trajectory around the moon while the other proposal would be to deliver the first module of Lunar Gateway into lunar orbit.

Preparations

Due to Artemis I's heatshield investigation, stacking the SLS rocket couldn't begin until it concluded contributing to a two year delay. The four engines were integrated into the core stage September 25, 2023 which was delivered to the KSC the end of July 2024. Stacking for the SLS rocket began November 2024 and concluded October 2025.

In April 2024, NASA held a news conference discussing their findings from the Artemis I heatshield and outlined changes they will make. Hot gasses got trapped in the AVCOAT material which caused cracking and unexpected pieces falling off. Because the heatshield was already integrated onto Orion, taking it out and replacing would lead to further delays than if they just decided to keep it on. NASA's decision was to keep the existing heatshield but change the reentry profile. Artemis I used what was called a skip-reentry profile where it initially enters the atmosphere, uses aerodynamic drag to slow down bouncing out of the atmosphere then reentering again at a much slower velocity. Artemis II used a more direct modified version of that profile. Orion entered the atmosphere and lifted up slightly to reduce its velocity but never left the atmosphere. By doing this the heatshield did experience higher heating but reduced the gas buildup.
